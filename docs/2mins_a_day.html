Overview

Kline's Keep Up Your Biblical Hebrew in Two Minutes a Day is a daily selection of verses, in Hebrew and English, selected to (re-)build your vocabulary.

AuthorJohnathan Kline, PhD
Year2019
PublisherHendrickson Publishers
CategorySupplement

Details

What does reading this feel like?

Each day, a full page presents a new bible verse in English and Hebrew. Verses are chosen to demonstrate a new vocabulary word, which is highlighted and defined. The verse is then broken down into segments where each is translated individually, helping you to parse the Hebrew, and complimenting the more fluid English translation above.

Vocabulary words are chosen in decreasing frequencies, starting with the conjunction vav on Day 1 with 50,524 occurrances and finishing with lakad (to catch, capture) on Day 365 with 121 occurrances. The frequency is listed along with the number from Strong's Concordance.

In addition to the word introduced daily, two other vocabulary words are highlighted--one typically from a more recent day and one from a bit farther back, helping to reinforce your memory. Pages are provided for the older words in case you need a refresher.

What all is included?

Kline provides one page per day, along with a preface and index.

The preface recommends three reading strategies, ranging from ten seconds to twenty minutes. Personallly, I tend to average fifteen minutes per day.

The index provides a mapping by book and verse to the day that references that verse.

The books is bound in beautiful leather with two bookmark ribbons.

What choices does the author make?

Kline sources the Hebrew text from the Michigan-Claremont-Westminster Electronic Hebrew Bible (based on the BHS), while he sources the English from six different translations. The Masoretic accents are absent from the Hebrew, and qere is used in place of ketiv.

For self-study students

Because of the focused, frequency-based vocabulary and segmented translation, even new students of Hebrew can benefit from this volume. In fact, this presents a nice opportunity for new students to begin reading real Hebrew immediately, even while their comprehension is still growing.

While page real estate is in short supply, students of all ages would have benefited from additional grammatical information, and this loss is more strongly felt by those without a teacher. Interestingly, this shortcoming is addressed in another of Kline's publications--A Proverb A Day in Biblical Hebrew, 2019, Hendrickson Publishers--where all verbs are listed with their root, form (e.g. "Inf Cst + prep") and binyan.

Going beyond

If you enjoy this format, Kline provides a second volume in Hebrew--for a total of two years of vocabulary--as well as two volumes in Greek, and one in Aramaic. Another of Kline's works mentioned above, A Proverb A Day in Biblical Hebrew, has a slightly different but comparable format and audience.

Rating

Indispensible.

If you have made the substantial investment of learning Biblical Hebrew, Greek, or Aramaic, then it only makes sense to preserve that investment. Two minutes daily is roughly the time it takes to brush your teeth. Doesn't the hard work you've put in merit that investment?

This is a rare book where the entire audience (i.e. students of Hebrew) should own a copy.
